## Part 2. The contribution loop: how a score or camera contribution becomes a revision

Nothing new is added to the structure; three existing fields gain one line each, and the return and adoption rules are written down. The seats are role-based (the music department's pattern): author and gate Ziggy; writing lead Claude; a contributor whoever Ziggy assigns for a pass (score drafter, cinematography contributor, pacing contributor); intake and delivery CFA Nova; the site index for display. Nothing here activates a seat or generates audio.

**2.1 Cue link (one line added to 11.5).** `Cue:` the cue ID and revision, or the fixed master; `state:` proposed cue, or generated and not listened, or listened and aligned; `relationship:` picture follows a music event, or score supports a fixed visual action, or timing moves together; `fixed:` what does not move; `flexible:` what may. **2.3 Musical purpose and arc (one line added to 11.6).** `Arc:` what the music does across the shot, then `so` the visible consequence, then whether the action `leads`, `lands with` or `follows` the cue; motif, tempo, event and hold provisional until listened and aligned. Examples of the form, not directions: tension gathers, so hold the eyeline; a release supports the reveal, the push and the turn; the sound falls away, so camera and actor settle. The arc is written in the block before any audio exists; it is corrected, never silently moved, when alignment lands.

**2.4 The contribution return (one shape for any seat).** A contribution is a proposal against a shot ID and its parent revision (file and sha256), stating: the sections changed (2 LIGHTING, 3 CAMERA, 4 CHARACTER DIRECTION, 5 BACKGROUND ACTION, 6 TIME BEATS; or a cue); the proposal, as the revised section text or a targeted delta (before and after); the reason; affected continuity and references (adjacent shots, the axis, locks, assets, music); the author (seat and registry id); what must remain. A score drafter's cue revision states the cue ID and revision, the affected shot IDs, the intended visible consequence and the cue's state. Proposals stay proposals beside the block and are shown to Ziggy through the site's queue; identity and story choices in the decision record are not a contributor's to change, and alternatives are preserved as alternatives.

**2.5 Adoption is a new creative revision, not a comment.** When Ziggy adopts a contribution, the writer reconciles it across 2 to 6, the beats table first, then regenerates 7 and 11.10 from that same revision, then refreshes the adapters that consume them. The result is take N plus 1 beside take N; the prior take keeps its sections 8 to 10 and every result attached to it. A comment beside an old copy-ready prompt is not integration. The revision names which pending prompt or source packs now need refreshing (the clip pack, the Flow brief, the Grok request, the Comfy intake). A camera change raises two checks before dispatch: does the prepared start frame still fit the new frame, and does the axis still hold across the adjacent shots.

**2.6 Two labels, kept apart.** A controlled test is "take N (A/B: one variable: ...)", one change against the prior take, scored against it; the head-to-head's rounds are A/B by construction. An adopted set of coupled improvements is "revision <letter> (creative revision: coupled changes: ...)", scored as a whole. The two never share a take, so a coupled artistic improvement can be judged coherently and a single variable can be measured cleanly.

**2.7 Audio layers never merged.** The song master (the edit's, never regenerated, never mouthed unless a card says to-lens and timing is accepted); ambience and effects (proposed in 11.6); provider audio (returned, recorded, unused unless chosen); a department cue (Lisan). Music intent is translated into visible action and pacing words in 7 and 11.10; no video surface is assumed to ingest or synchronize to an audio file; where a route exposes an audio input the operator binds it as `audio_reference`, and actual synchronization evidence is recorded in section 9 after the return, never before.

## Part 3. Scope A is an improvement hand-off, for whoever is assigned

**What changed in the ask.** Ziggy's clarification, relayed by the operator in substance: the storyboards are handed to collaborators whose job is to improve them. Scope A is that hand-off, provider-neutral: a notebook is one possible recipient, a model with repository read access another, a person a third. The clip-scoped one-source rule of October 14 belongs to scope B, video execution; it is not a restriction on story development. An improvement contributor receives the sequence or the story the assignment needs: a camera reviewer sees the adjacent shots and the axis; a score reviewer sees the cue arc and the sequence; a pacing reviewer sees the spine, the cut logic and the beats tables.
