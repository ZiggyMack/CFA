## Why one card

The song plays its hook five times and the words change twice: the first chorus names a price, the three middle ones ask why he turned the ship around, and the last one answers with the price paid. The board drew three different "Dolo performs" panels for it. The pass's rule is the opposite: the counter from the opening prototype is the chorus picture (S01, S03, S11), and each return to it adds one change, so the repetition reads as a change in the man and not a cut back to B-roll. The two middle choruses that leave the counter (S06 to frame C, S09 to frame B) are the two changes of place, and inside those frames the same rule holds: one picture, one change. This card writes the five changes and the outro that follows them. Frame A is the reviewed ten-turn prototype's locked medium-wide and is not redesigned here; the card only says what each return to it adds.

Constraints carried from the record: the prototype is silent visual acting with no lip-sync inside the ten-turn test, and the lip-sync for S01A, S03A, S09A and S11A is a separate editorial layer on the plan's rap_lipsync cards; SB-01 stays an extreme mouth close-up editorial insert separate from the continuous prototype; mouth external-off throughout the song; the four credits, the unlettered screen-right indicator and the provisional character-right wrist placement are the prototype's controls; Leia's reverse at the counter is one beat and the only place her face appears before S04 (S02-1 shows her hand); Ziggy's answer on the outro was "restrained, for now" (DECISIONS section 3, row 4), and this card's reading of restrained (the three names arrive as sound, no desert, no title card inside the song, no Lisan crossover imagery) is a proposal; no Vader before Verse 3.

## Cut logic across the choruses

Every chorus returns to a frame the song has already been in, at the angle it left: S01, S03 and S11 to frame A; S06 to frame C (the console, as S04-7 left it); S09 to frame B (the cockpit, as S08-4b left it). The rule: a chorus never introduces a place the song has not already been. The five changes, in order: the price (nothing changes; it is the baseline), the glance (his eyes), Chewie's amusement (the copilot's eyes), the battle light (the light on him), the identical hold against the slab (the edit). The outro changes nothing; it holds.
