## 2. The loop, now that it is the same settlement **[proposed]**

**The shape.** The light that answers Lisan at the tower (Story 01, scene 9) is the settlement's own tower forty years on, the lamps of the people he taught, reaching back through the relay. Episode 7 "The Answer" is therefore not a crossing to another settlement; it is a crossing in time without leaving the ridge, and the "first exchange between communities" of the series plan's Episode 7 row (Seed 04's "first exchange of what each learned that the other had not") becomes the first exchange between the settlement and itself. Two consequences, both labeled: Jet knew and did not say (the teacher withholds the outcome, New_11 B6); and Lisan learns the answer was his own and that he will not live to send it, which is the cost the rule of the well demands.

**The loop.** The time well's useful shape (from *Dark*, ideas only: nothing of its names, people, places, devices, plot or coinages) is the closed loop where the message that starts the story is sent at its end, and the same people stand at both ends at different ages. The series already has the pieces: the thousand portraits were painted before he came; the city woke the thousandth when the water came back; the Keepers sealed the scoop forty years ago; the storm wall crosses forty storms. So: the forty years is the loop's length. The half-blueprint man opened the scoop forty years ago on half a message; the whole message is the one the settlement sends back forty years after Lisan opens it whole. The message that killed three was the first half of the one that saved the vault, received too early. That is the series' spine of time in one sentence, and it needs no machine.

**The same people at both ends.** Angels at the rack, forty years on, is the old man who taught the thousandth to walk on sand (Angels' note, Episode 9); Sassy at the fire is the one still setting Jet's cup; the child who drew the valve in the sand (Story 01, scene 8) is the one at the relay key, sending. The Keepers' oldest is the one who understood late and kept the tower lit. These are the lamps that blink back.

## 3. The two futures in one mirror, and the Fall **[proposed, on Ziggy's answer 4]**

The mirror shows two futures and the series never says which is real until Lisan pays. In one, the light answers (Episode 7): the settlement forty years on, lit. In the other, the devil in the mirror with Lisan's eyes (MM_0's coda; Episode 8): Lisan forty years on, having become the thing the Keepers were afraid of, the false prophet, the one who kept the knowing for himself once he had it whole. Same face, same forty years, two threadings of the needle.

**The rule Ziggy set, as the writer applies it:** the Fall is a possibility and never an inevitability; it comes only from a failure on Lisan's part; Jet reminds him of it constantly and warns him to be careful. So Jet gains a refrain. Every return, after the lesson or the message, one line of warning, paraphrased each time and never the same words, to the effect that the whole blueprint in one man's hands is still half a blueprint. The Nyquist well already names the failure's shape (THE_FLOOD_GATES for the Awakener's arc as a problem, the nihilist and the cynic as what he could become; the Gnostic-1-2-x3 digest's Permanent Fall, from Enoch, as the gloved hand's reason; the codex's Walking Dead, attributed) and the Castaneda well names its sign (the healthy shift laughs after horror; the failed one turns righteous, New_11 B3). The series' own test for which future he is in: whether he still hands the knowing on, or starts to ration it. The Keepers rationed water; the fallen Lisan would ration the light.

**Where the devil enters:** Episode 4 "Inside the Storm" already has the Keepers' bargain arriving "in his own voice"; it is now the fallen Lisan's voice, from the branch where he took the bargain, the one crossing that is a warning from a worse future (Seed 09's table). Episode 8's mirror shows him the face. Episode 10's witness decides nothing; the made minds watch both and the series ends on the one Lisan paid for.

**The desert series' canon, the slight tweak Ziggy allows:** the idea of a manufactured messiah and of a prophecy that becomes a trap is the well's idea and stays an idea; our tweak is that the trap is not the prophecy but the man's own hand, and that it can be refused. Nothing of that series' people, places, plot or devices comes in.
