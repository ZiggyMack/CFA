## 2. The event arc (every event placed; the music tracks these and nothing else)

1. **The camp below.** Night, the storm gone over; the fire's glow and full cups under the ridge (the end of scene 8 seen from above as he climbs). Quiet; the fire's warmth is the only warm light.
2. **The climb.** The dead tower on the ridge, a thing the Keepers also kept; starlight; his hands on cold iron. Slow. No tempo yet, or a tempo so slow it reads as breath.
3. **The lattice shows him the relay.** The light opens by the relay (its colour unrecorded; the music invents no colour). The first structural sound: something that was not there is now there, as the lattice was in the vault.
4. **The key thrown.** One mechanical event: a throw, a contact. The cue's one hard edge, small and dry. It is the scene's own sound and stays audible; the music makes room for it, as it made room for the thumper's knock.
5. **Noise.** The relay's lamps come on and its noise fills the air: static, hiss, the sound of a dead thing powered. The music does not compete with it; the music is under it or inside it. The relay's lamps are the tower's own light (S01-9.2) and join the emitters of §3. Nothing is answering yet.
6. **Inside the noise, a light.** Far out on the dunes, a light blinking in the same rhythm his lamps blinked when the pump came up. The cue's turn, and it is not a reveal sting: a rhythm appears where there was texture. The rhythm is given by the picture of S01-9.3, the far light's blink, which the story ties to his lamps when the pump came up (the story says no more than that about scene 7's lamps); the music may take it as its one pulse, never replace it with its own.
7. **Jet on the rig's roof.** Below, Jet watches the horizon, the cube turning by the gloved hand. He has not moved; he does not react. The music does not give him a motif of his own here; he is part of the night.
8. **The last frame: two hands.** Lisan's on the key, grit under the nails; Jet's, gloved, at his side. **The one note lands here and holds to black.** No fade, no swell, no chord that resolves the piece; one note, held, cut.

## 4. The rules this cue carries

- **Ends on one note (the series plan's words).** A single held tone from the two hands to black. Not a fade, not a resolution, not a swell. The three directions below each say what the note is made of.
- **Answers, does not announce.** The light is, by Ziggy's answer 1 and the writer's **[proposed]** loop (`TIME_AND_THE_FALL` §1 and §2), the settlement's own tower forty years on; Story 01's own label still reads "a second settlement", and the reading is proposed, not record. The music must not telegraph this: no future theme, no reprise that says "you will hear this again", no uncanny sting. The audience of Story 01 hears a reply and nothing more. What the music may do is rhyme with the vault cue's release: in DeepSeek's two drafts that release is a warm bloom as the lamps come up, with no pulse of its own, so a rhythm placed there (a writer's suggestion for the vault card, Ziggy's call) would be the rhythm the far light blinks here, and if Episode 7 returns to the ridge the two cues already rhyme. That rhyme is a suggestion, not a rule.
- **Follows two cues, answers both.** The sandworm beat ended on a held reveal (the man on the dune); the vault ended on a release (the breath, the lamps). This cue ends on neither: on a single note, the smallest ending of the three. If the three cues share any material, it is the lamps (the vault's release, event 9 there, where no rhythm is yet placed; the tower's far light, event 6 here) and nothing else.
- **Score-song rules (series plan).** A score song carries no lyric cue in the storyboard, so none of the fifty panels is re-mapped to this cue; if the piece has words, the scene is cut so that no mouth on screen is singing them; scene 9 has no spoken line, so a voice, if Ziggy wants one, is off-screen by construction. The writer's default is no voice at all: 45 s is too short to place a line and the far light is the only reply the scene allows.
- **No triumph.** The lamps answered; nothing is won. A man on a cold tower has been heard, once.

## 5. Direction A — the climb as breath (strings and air)

A slow piece for a man climbing a dead radio tower at night above a desert camp. Long bowed tones in the low and middle register, moving at the pace of breath, with air between them; the fire below is a faint warmth in the lowest voice. The lattice's light opens by the relay as one high sustained harmonic entering, structural and still. The key is thrown: one dry mechanical contact, close, and the music steps back from it. The relay's noise fills the scene; the strings thin to almost nothing underneath, so the noise is the piece for a moment. Inside the noise a far light begins to blink, and a soft repeated pulse appears in the music in that rhythm, no louder than the noise. Below, a figure on a rig roof watches the horizon; the music gives him nothing of his own. The pulse stops; one middle-register bowed note is left, held, unresolved, and cut to black.

Copy-ready prompt:

```text
Instrumental cinematic score, slow and spare, about 45 seconds, for a man climbing a dead radio tower at night above a desert camp. Long bowed string tones in the low and middle register move at the pace of breath, with silence between them; a faint warm low drone is the fire far below. A single high sustained harmonic enters as a lattice of light opens beside the relay. One dry, close mechanical click as a key is thrown, and the music steps back. Radio static and hiss take the scene while the strings thin to almost nothing. Inside the static a soft repeated pulse appears, like a far light blinking in a slow steady rhythm, no louder than the noise. The pulse stops. One middle-register bowed note is left alone, held, unresolved, and ends cleanly without fading. No vocals, no lyrics, no drums, no melody, no triumphant swell, no reverb tail at the end.
```

## 6. Direction B — the relay as the instrument (electro-acoustic)

A piece built from the scene's own sounds. The climb is near-silence: wind on iron, a low electrical hum under the ridge that is not yet the relay. The lattice opening by the relay is a clean tone arriving inside the hum. The key is thrown and the hum becomes noise: the relay's static shaped into the body of the cue, filtered slowly so that it breathes, never musical in itself. Inside it a pulse appears, a filtered blink in the rhythm of the lamps, and the noise organises itself around that pulse for a few bars without ever becoming a beat. The figure on the rig roof gets no sound. The noise is cut away in one move, and what is left is one clean electronic tone, the lattice's tone from the start, held to black.

Copy-ready prompt:

```text
Electro-acoustic instrumental score, about 45 seconds, built from the sounds of a dead radio tower at night in a desert. Near-silence to begin: wind on iron, a low electrical hum. A single clean sustained tone arrives inside the hum. One close dry click as a key is thrown, and the hum turns to radio static and hiss, slowly filtered so that it swells and breathes like a body, never musical in itself. Inside the static a soft filtered pulse appears, a far light blinking in a slow steady rhythm; the noise gathers around the pulse without becoming a beat. Then the static is cut away in one move, leaving the single clean tone from the beginning, held alone, unresolved, and ending cleanly without fading. No vocals, no lyrics, no drums, no melody, no cinematic swell.
```

## 7. Direction C — one instrument (unlike A's strings and B's noise)

One player, one instrument, alone for the whole cue, the way the series plan's Episode 2 silence lesson proposes a song that strips itself down to one instrument. A plucked or bowed low instrument (a cello played pizzicato, or a long-necked lute; the choice is Ziggy's) plays a descending line of five or six notes once during the climb, slowly, and then waits. The lattice opens: the player holds one high note under the breath. The key is thrown: silence from the instrument; the relay's noise alone. The far light blinks: the player answers it with one plucked note per blink, in its rhythm, no more than the picture gives. Jet on the roof: nothing. The last frame: one bowed open string, held, cut.

Copy-ready prompt:

```text
Solo instrumental score for a single low string instrument, about 45 seconds, for a man climbing a dead radio tower at night in a desert. A slow descending line of five or six plucked notes, played once with long silences, then stillness. One high bowed note held quietly as a light opens. Then the instrument falls silent and only room tone remains for several seconds. Then single plucked notes return, one at a time, in a slow steady rhythm like a far light blinking, sparse and dry, exactly in that rhythm and nothing added. The plucking stops. One bowed open string is held alone, unresolved, and ends cleanly without fading. No other instruments, no vocals, no lyrics, no drums, no effects, no swell.
```
