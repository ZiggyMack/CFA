## 2. The spine, confirmed and sharpened

The pass proposed two worlds, one face: the desert as the outer world where his body is, the mirror as the inner world where he is everyone, the mirror as the only door, one change per return. The text says it in its first three lines: "Voice from the outer world, / Calling through the mirror / Voice from the inner world". And again at the end of Verse 3: "Outer world, inner world—same breath, two doors." The reading stands; it is now the song's own.

Three sharpenings:

1. **The returns have lines.** The mirror returns are the Chorus (panels 01–08), the Refrain (no panels; the return frame with one change), and the Final Refrain (no panels until "Find the signal"; the last return, "We do."). MM_0 adds a fourth, the short refrain inside Verse 1, and the "Under the lies" line after the Chorus. The mirror card (card 3 in the pass's order) now plans three returns in set 1 and four in set 2, each with one change, instead of "each chorus". The one change per return can follow the text's own escalation: the Refrain's whispered "who's meta mirrors all?" and the Final Refrain's answer "We do."
2. **The sandworm beat has its lines.** Bridge 1 is exactly "Lisan, to the silence break. / Buried deep Al-Gaib / Cooking in the pan, one for every womb and man" (twice in set 1). The silence breaks on the thumper; the maw rises on "Buried deep Al-Gaib"; the pan line, twice, is the length of the hold. Bridge 2 repeats "Lisan, to the silence break. / Buried deep Al-Gaib" before "don't fall": a second desert beat, the tracks or the worm's wake, before panel 23's ledge. The sandworm card gains a reprise shot there (proposal; the card as posted is unchanged).
3. **The ending card has its lines, and Jet is in the song.** "Outer world, inner world—same breath, two doors. / Thought I came to wake you. / Had to wake up first." is TD-1 and TD-2: Verse 3 was the waking up; the walk to the sun door is "came to wake you". Then "Code or a pulse—who's there on the line? / Got a question? Pull up. We got time." is a voice on a line that may be code or a person: that is Jet's function in one couplet (the watcher who personifies Codex) and the radio tower's lyric (TD-4's desert twin). Then "Lisan on the mic… ain't speakin' from a throne. / PanHandlers in the mirror— / Nobody gotta think alone." is panel 49. The Final Refrain is the last mirror return; "Find the signal in the noise— / Lisan Al Gaib / Voice from the outer world" is panel 50. The two sets give two different last frames: set 1 ends on "one for every womb and man…." (the hook; the people, C45 or C31, as the last picture), set 2 ends on "I kept pointin' at the devil— / Why he got my eyes?" (TD-5, the eye in macro). The two-doors card's two candidate last frames turn out to be the two versions' endings.

## 3. The hook is a picture

"Cooking in the pan, one for every womb and man" is the song's spine line. It occurs in Bridge 1 (twice in set 1, once in set 2), in Verse 3 after "You brought me here", and twice in the Final Refrain, then once more as the last line. Proposal: it gets one recurring picture, a one-second cut-in, the same every time, the glowing cast-iron pan of panel 04's kitchen-studio or the camp kitchen of panel 11, so the brand's heartbeat is visible each time it is heard. In Track B the same line is the blueprint's meaning: "one for every womb and man" is what "freely available to everyone" sounds like sung. Story 01 can carry it without a change.

## 4. The panels that read the slang literally (for Ziggy, not for repair)

The text names people; the panels drew things. Panel 14 shows a muscular dog in a gold chain for "that's my dog dipped in flavor", but the line is about Kaleb K, the lawyer "on retainer". Panel 15 shows Lisan at a vanity mirror for "baddest in the mirror", but the line is about a woman, Sassy Frass, "the queen", saluted with "Salute, mama - auf Wiedersehen" (panel 18 keeps the salute). Panel 30 shows a canyon echo for "Shout out to Echo", a name. Panel 31 shows vinyl for "another in the chamber, - load another one". None of these is wrong for a music video; literal pictures of slang are a style. But they are decisions: whether Kaleb K, Sassy Frass, CJ ("CJ! (Echoed adlib)"), Echo and Naga ("Naga we got shadow work to do") are real people or personas who appear, or stay names in the air. On the Hon Dolo side the informant was such a decision.
