## 1. The sequence as one thought

A man who charges for everything is paid in information. The prologue shows the only transaction in the piece where no money changes hands, and the chorus that follows is him turning that information into a price. Everything in the cab is small: one passenger, one glance, one half-smile. Everything at the counter is the same man at full volume. The cut between them is the song starting.

Motivation, for the actors in the frame. Dolo drives the cab because nothing else is moving tonight; it is not beneath him, it is Tuesday. The cab is his (DECISIONS section 2: the informant boards Dolo's cab), so it goes where he goes and waits where he leaves it. He drives with one hand. He does not look at passengers, because passengers are fares. The informant has no fare and knows it before she boards; she is not nervous about it, because she has something worth more and she knows that too. She never sells the tip. She states it once, flat, looking at him, and lets him do the arithmetic. His glance at her (P-5) is the arithmetic finishing. The half-smile that ends the glance is the same half-smile the prototype arrives at over the counter ("nice try", turn 9, before the settle): the prologue plants it, the chorus pays it. That is the one performance thread this packet asks the two scenes to share, and it costs nothing.

Why a counter and not the ship: the tip is about a woman with money, so the next thing he does is go where she will be. The cantina is where rides are sold. He walks in already selling.
