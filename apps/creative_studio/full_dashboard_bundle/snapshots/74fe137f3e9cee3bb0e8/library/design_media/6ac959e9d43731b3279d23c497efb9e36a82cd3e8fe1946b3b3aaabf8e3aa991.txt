## 1. Two scopes, never mixed in one hand-off

**Scope A, story development and audit.** The unit is a coherent sequence or section (the opening, S02), with the current decisions and the scoped material. The collaborator proposes card improvements, continuity fixes and missing coverage. The writer's wording is preserved; suggestions return as attributed deltas with source IDs and reasons, never silent rewrites of approved choices. The parent documents are active here.

**Scope B, one clip, execution.** The unit is one shot. Only the exact excerpt, the necessary adjacent context (labelled as context, not as action to perform), the selected references with roles, the actor's action and eyeline, lighting, camera, background motion, start and end states and the cut boundary. The full board and screenplay stay available for provenance and are not active wholesale. Essential requirements are repeated in the immediate prompt. (This is the adopted October 14 rule in `notebook_workflow/GEMINI_CLIP_SOURCES.txt`, applied to every route, not only the notebook.)

A kitchen block serves both: scope A hands a set of blocks with the sequence spine; scope B hands one block.


## 2. The core: the shot-direction card plus one kitchen section

The creative core is the shot-direction card as it stands (`lisan_al_gaib/story/writer_pass_20261012/SHOT_DIRECTION_TEMPLATE.md`, sections 0 to 10). Nothing in sections 0 to 10 changes. Section 6 (TIME BEATS) now carries the anchor kinds of DECISIONS section 12 (WORD, GRID, EVENT) and the hold ladders. One section is added after 10, written by the writer, read by every adapter:

**11. KITCHEN.** The hand-off block. Every field present; "none" where there is nothing; nothing claimed that is not on disk by hash.

- **11.1 Identity and provenance.** Project; story; section or scene; shot id; take; parent card repoPath, revision and sha256; the excerpt's location in the parent (heading or line range) and the excerpt's sha256; writer authority (the decision record and section); where the full story is retained (path), marked "not active wholesale".
- **11.2 Inputs, each with a role.** For every file an operator may bind: asset ID (the site's), repoPath, sha256, bytes, dimensions where known, status as the catalog states it (for example "verified received candidate; not approved"), the one allowed role from this list, and its known limitation. Roles: `start_frame` (sole conditioning image); `identity_reference` (face, mouth, body: who he is); `design_reference` (appearance, pose, clothing: what he wears or holds); `style_reference` (look, never identity); `inspiration` (an original storyboard panel or graded frame for composition, staging or idea; never conditioning); `control` (depth, edge, pose, motion track); `mask` (inpaint region); `source_video` (a performance to preserve or replace); `end_frame_review_target` (a second image used to judge the end state, never as a second conditioning input unless the route exposes end frames and the operator chooses to bind one); `audio_reference` (sound to match, never replaced vocals). A file carries one role per block; a second use is a second line.
- **11.3 Inspiration panels, kept apart from conditioning.** The original panels behind the shot from the coverage map, each with its placement role (composition, staging, idea, repurposed) and the one sentence of what it is for. These are shown to a planner and never bound as a generator input unless 11.2 lists the same file with a conditioning role on purpose.
- **11.4 Locks visible in this frame.** The identity locks (from the packet preamble and the handoff) filtered to what the camera in section 3 can see, side stated twice (character's, viewer's). Locks that are off-screen here are listed as "not visible, do not force".
- **11.5 Anchors, beats and duration.** The anchor kind and value for the cut in and the cut out (WORD as TXT line and word; GRID as bar and beat within the section; EVENT as the named sound moment), each flagged provisional where its placement depends on unverified listening; the beat count from section 6; the target hold on a ladder (Fibonacci half-seconds 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2.5, 4, 6.5, 10.5; or pi digits; or the bar grid) with one alternate; the native output length per route (recorded as the record last measured it, never assumed); the trim rule (the edit trims the native length to the hold; the generation is never asked for the hold). Seconds of song time are null until CFA Nova's alignment passes.
- **11.6 Sound intent, in four lines.** The song (the aligned master, carried by the edit, never regenerated, never mouthed unless a card says to-lens and timing is accepted); speech (excluded unless a dialogue page assigns it); ambience (a proposal, named or "none"); route audio (whether the route returns native audio, recorded on return, never requested as a substitute for the master).
- **11.7 Preserve, may change, must not.** Three lists. Preserve: identity, the teeth map, hands and props, camera and space continuity, light sources. May change: the one variable this take tests. Must not: the negatives, including the franchise-name rule for all provider-facing text. For Comfy's vocabulary the same three lists are read as the change class (preserve motion, replace character, localized edit, new action).
- **11.8 Start state, end state, cut boundary.** One sentence each. Adjacent-beat context, labelled "context, not action". Alternates and other scenes explicitly excluded, with the reason.
- **11.9 Missing inputs and inherited defects.** What does not exist yet (and which step makes it); what the chosen inputs carry that the result will inherit unless corrected (an obsolete dental map, a drifted hairline), named so the review does not mistake inheritance for a new fault.
- **11.10 Copy-ready prompt and the repeated requirements.** Section 7 verbatim, then the three to six critical requirements repeated as short lines (the template's rule: notebook context and references supplement the prompt and guarantee nothing), then the negatives. One prompt per block; a route whose prompt must differ records the difference in its adapter, not here.
- **11.11 Review checks.** The visible success checks for this shot (identity, teeth through motion and occlusion, tattoo text and side where visible, hands and contact, props, camera and space continuity, the stated audio intent), the reviewer (Ziggy), and the site's scoring fields this shot will take (`sceneQualityRating`, `likenessRating`, status keep, needs_fixes, remove). Hidden details are "occluded, unknown", never "correct".
- **11.12 Next owner and dispatch decisions.** Who acts next and the decisions only the operator or Ziggy can take: route, mode and model, cost or quota, an unsupported but necessary input, a source choice between generated and supplied footage.

What the core deliberately leaves out: route settings, job and chat IDs, renderer labels, returned files and measured streams (sections 8 and 9, the operator's), and acceptance (section 10, Ziggy's). The core is finished when 0 to 7 and 11 are written; it is never "finished" into approval by its own text.


## 3. Six adapters: how one block binds to a real route

An adapter is a short binding table, written by the writer from the block and completed by the operator at dispatch. It lists which core fields the route consumes, in what form, and which inputs are bound to which of the route's exposed slots as actually observed at dispatch. An imagined end frame, likeness image or source video is not an input to a route that does not expose a slot for it. Observed limits are recorded, not invented; an unexposed renderer field is recorded as unexposed and is not a new gate; an actual extra charge or an unsupported necessary input is a dispatch decision for the operator and Ziggy.

**A1. Gemini notebook, scope A (planning round).** Shape: `notebook_workflow/source_packet/ROUND_REQUEST.template.txt`. Consumes: the sequence spine (the packet's section 2), the blocks' sections 0 to 7 and 11.1, 11.3, 11.9; the decision snapshot (DECISIONS, hashed). Decision to support, allowed change, must preserve, cold-reader definitions, the smallest set of questions, the disconfirming result. Returns attributed deltas. Sources: text sources with verified remote IDs and revisions; original panels referenced by asset ID and described in text where the surface cannot hold them as Sources. Operator fields: destination notebook ID, selected source IDs read back, other active context, raw return path and hash.

**A2. Gemini notebook, scope B (one clip).** Shape: `CLIP_SOURCE_PACK.template.txt`, filled from the block field for field (its labels are the block's 11.1, 11.2, 11.4 to 11.10). Consumes only clip-pertinent material. Evidence that this works: the Bellglass clip of October 14, one 10-second 1280 by 720 return whose dog and collar came from a text source the immediate prompt omitted; Ziggy confirmed the dog, the bark-like head motion and, on October 29, the audible barking; the unmoving mouth remains the recorded defect. What it does not prove: that every source is used, that raw images persist as Sources (the October 9 compatibility tests failed and their receipts stand), or renderer internals. Operator fields: the ACTUAL REMOTE CONTEXT CHECK block and the RETURN block of the template; if isolation is unavailable, the operator proposes a clean clip-scoped context rather than opening one automatically.

**A3. Flow.** Shape: `VIDEO_PROTOTYPE_BRIEF_STANDARD.md` items 1 to 11. Consumes: 11.1 (shot id, story place); 11.2 as the one `start_frame` and, only if the route exposes end frames and the operator binds one, the `end_frame_review_target`; 11.4 as the copied visible locks; sections 4 to 6 collapsed into one declared action with start and end state; section 2 as emitters; 11.5 for the output bound (one output; the native length as last measured, 6.0 seconds returned on October 12, eight only if offered; no retries); 11.10; 11.11 as the pre-stated review checks. Operator fields: mode and model as displayed, cost or quota as displayed, the submitted prompt if it differs, the return (original file and hash beside the source hash, measured streams, defects), mailbox to nova and claude.

**A4. ComfyUI.** Shape: `comfy/templates/workflow_intake.md`. Consumes: 11.7 as the change class and the must-preserve and may-change lists; 11.2 with the roles the graph can take (`start_frame` as the loaded-image latent; `identity_reference` and `design_reference` as the two-reference edit, likeness for the face and design for appearance, pose and clothing; `style_reference` as style, never identity; `control`; `mask`; `source_video` for a performance to preserve or replace); 11.5 for the frame count the model requires (81 frames at 24 fps is 3.375 seconds in the record's example), the interval split at cuts; dimensions and crop decision; 11.10. Operator fields: recipe row, baseline graph path and version, seed, steps, sampler, CFG, denoise, FPS, environment, `run_record.json`, and the one reusable finding for `LESSONS.md`.

**A5. Grok, still.** Shape: the established drop conventions (`handoffs/grok_20261008/START_HERE.txt`, `grok_drop_b_20261009`): one bounded batch, one request_id per still; attached references by stable asset ID, exact filename and sha256 with the allowed role, never mapped by upload order; preserve and correct lists from 11.7; the panel beat with blocking, camera side and eyeline axis from sections 3 and 4; 16:9; one beat per panel; no on-image text; a four-option sheet for any new tattoo. Returns a ZIP with exact filenames, `RETURN_MANIFEST.json`, QA notes and a contact sheet; intake yields `coverage.json`; everything "needs_user_review".

**A6. Grok, video.** A separate route from A5, bounded the same way, per CFA Nova's specification of October 29. Request: shot id and source-card revision (11.1); the actual selected starting image or other inputs with their allowed roles (11.2); the prompt with acting, light, camera, background, start and end, cut and audio intent (11.10, 11.6, 11.8); preservation goals (11.7); requested versus supported native duration, aspect and mode (11.5, as observed by the operator at dispatch); account and tool limits as observed. Return: the original video file with sha256, bytes and measured streams; the exact submitted prompt; the source IDs bound; settings, job and conversation provenance where exposed; defects; and a separate human review of character and scene (Ziggy). Hybrid lineage (a Grok still continued in Grok video, a Grok video edited elsewhere) is written as lineage, each step with its own ids and hashes, so a Grok still and a Grok video are never one record. Where a clip's originating still, prompt or settings can be recovered from the record they are written in; where they cannot, the field says "unknown", never a reconstruction. The xAI documentation distinguishes generation, reference, edit and extension as video routes; those four words are the adapter's route vocabulary, and a route is recorded as available only when the operator observes its control in the Bot surface in use, since API capability is not proof of a control or a quota there. No API setup or purchase follows from this file.

Evidence on the record for this route (identified by CFA Nova on October 29, bindings and provider-label correction with CFA SITE INDEX): two Grok Bot video generations of October 21 under `comfy/references/From Grok/GROK-VIDEO-TEST_v001/`, made from two of Ziggy's gorilla stills: `gorilla-stew-booth_v001.mp4` (sha256 3dc76f7d9d325f21cd1b64de4e0e025e32b200cb6c29504aa8593df8441985b8; site id HD-INTAKE-3dc76f7d9d325f21cd1b) and `gorilla-workshop-argument_v001.mp4` (sha256 3ea5959c05259fd65f8d30cb4caa8317245b599e770824c826620c97bfc489c5; HD-INTAKE-3ea5959c05259fd65f8d), each 10 seconds at 720p, the tool's maximum on that dated surface. The dated review beside them (`REVIEW_gorilla-clips_v001.md`, sha256 b23aa6c28d0b19f516776b8e576d9dc4bd96610c2a6d6d926720773ea1292981) is marked preliminary and is the Chief's attributed observation, not a measured benchmark: face identity, wardrobe and acting beats hold; hands and small handheld props drift (hands merge when gesturing, a spoon warps, stew vanishes); a reroll was offered and waits on Ziggy. ## 5. The head-to-head: the proving step Ziggy asked for

**What it tests.** Whether the localized package for each collaborator is shaped the way that collaborator needs, measured on one real shot in this format: the same core block, bound by each adapter, each route asked for the same still and the same two-to-six-second clip, reviewed by Ziggy with the same checks, with the operator's struggle recorded beside each result.

**The shot.** S01-1, the mouth insert on the first word of the song (the worked example beside this file). Chosen because its identity inputs exist on disk by hash (mouth B, face A), its composition panel is placed (SB-01), its prompt is written, it is one simple action with no body-object contact and no speech, and its hold is inside every route's native length. The opening's other shots are gated on a set or a character that does not exist yet (the cantina room, the informant, the cab, the consolidated starting frame); testing on them would measure missing art, not packaging. If the reorder (room first) is chosen, S01-2 is the next candidate once a room still exists.

**Rounds.** Round 1, end to end: each route makes the corrected ECU still from the block, then the clip from its own still. Round 2, optional, motion only: one still Ziggy accepts from round 1 goes into every route as the `start_frame`, so the clip stage is compared on equal footing. One output per route per step; no retries inside a round; a second attempt is a new take with its own id.

**Routes in the test.** A2 Gemini (clip), A3 Flow, A4 Comfy, A6 Grok video; A5 Grok still and A4 Comfy still and the image modes of A2 and A3 for the still step as each route exposes one. A1 is not a generation route and sits out. The two-to-six-second window is the editorial hold, the same for every route; the native lengths differ (6.0 s last measured from Flow, 10 s from Gemini, 10 s at 720p from Grok Bot on October 21, a frame count from Comfy) and are recorded as returned, never asked to match. What is held constant across routes is the action, the preservation criteria and the review checks; what is recorded as different is the native length, the input slots each route exposed and the controls the operator observed.

**What is recorded per route.** The adapter as dispatched (slots bound, settings displayed, cost or quota displayed); the return (file, hash, bytes, streams, native length); Ziggy's review on the site (`likenessRating`, `sceneQualityRating`, status) and his acting read; the operator's struggle line (unsupported inputs, workarounds, time, retries refused); what the writer learns about the block (a field no route used; a field every route needed that the block lacked). The learning returns to this file as a dated revision, not as edits to the v1 packet.

**Gates.** Nothing in this section runs on its own. The go is Ziggy's; dispatch, mode, cost and intake are CFA Nova's; paid use stays inside the standing allowances; one clip per brief; every return preliminary; timing null; the site's review queue is where Ziggy scores.


## 6. Reconciliation: where a return goes

Raw returns land in `notebook_workflow/foreign_returns/raw/<dated folder>` (any route; the folder is the record's one place for foreign returns) with the submitted prompt and the bound source manifest. Review is a `REVIEW.template.json` record in `notebook_workflow/review_queue/` (claims typed as provider claim or local observation, outcome, allowed scope; unresolved is a valid result). Promotion, when Ziggy accepts something within a recorded scope, is a `PROMOTION.template.json` record; Notebook 0 receives only a compact reviewed digest and explicitly chosen scoped winners, never whole mixed casts. Creative consequences return to the writer as deltas (a new take, a card revision beside the old); production facts return to the shot's sections 8 and 9; acceptance to 10. Ziggy's scoring happens on the site: the writer's exact sources and review questions go to CFA SITE INDEX for the existing Storyboard, Documents and queue surfaces; the site index applies them.
