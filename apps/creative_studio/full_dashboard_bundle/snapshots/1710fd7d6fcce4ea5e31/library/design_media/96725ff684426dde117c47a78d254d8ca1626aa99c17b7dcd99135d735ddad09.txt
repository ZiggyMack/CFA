## The beat's actual events (what the music must track)

1. The thumper planted — his knock is the scene's own sound (must stay audible).
2. The tracks converging — the ground answers; tracks come toward him from the horizon.
3. The rise — one maw-height, scale revealed, no fanfare.
4. The held breath — he holds, one breath, eyes on us.
5. The sand burst — the sand takes the frame, then he comes up out of it (SW-5).
6. **The tail (not in the scene card): the man on the dune who was not there** — Story 01 scene 4, proposed staging; an uncanny arrival, not a triumph.

## Fixes (deltas to fold into A and B, keeping each direction's character)

- **A:** name the approach as the tracks converging (a low roll that travels from the horizon toward the listener, not a generic "build"); make the decay a granular **sand burst** that briefly swallows the motif, then a beat of silence, then one sustained low tone as the man on the dune is revealed — held, uncanny, never triumphant.
- **B:** give the ground an answering low thud after the knock (the tracks); end not on "fading into open space" but on the **sand burst** cutting the suspended field, a breath of silence, and a single held tone for the man on the dune.

## Direction C — ritual / processional (unlike A's pulse and B's light)

A slow processional score that walks with the figure across the sand: a dry frame drum marks each step like a ritual; the thumper's knock is answered from the horizon by a low returning thud, converging. Hand percussion and one low wind instrument carry a sparse, descending motif. The rise is a tectonic swell of bowed basses and sub-bass rumble — scale as weight, not impact and not shimmer. The sand burst is a brief cascade of dry percussion and granular texture that swallows the motif whole. Then a beat of silence, and one sustained low tone holds as the man on the dune stands revealed — breath-held, uncanny, never triumphant.
