## 1. Notes on Grok's first pass

Credit first. The 36-panel board already has a spine: six series (cold open, bounty heat, Kessel run, turnaround, Carbon Knight, outro) that track the song's own escalation from fee to flight to sacrifice. Three motifs recur without being told to: the mouth (SB-01, SB-08, SB-26, SB-36), the hands on money or controls (SB-03, SB-14, SB-19, SB-23) and the copilot at the boards (SB-05, SB-18, SB-23, SB-32). Those are the right motifs, and the rest of this pass builds on them.

What the board gets wrong is already catalogued by CFA (identity drift, the ape and the bear, the horizontal chest ship, the 4:30 timeline). I will not repeat the likeness notes; panels guide composition, not identity. My notes are story notes.

1. **The board has no Leia until the farewell.** She is the center of the irony and the song puts her in the first Verse 2 (the junky home, the protection promise, the fee demanded in the same breath). The board jumps from the Kessel run to the turnaround. Series 03 or 04 needs her arrival and her look around the ship. This is the biggest missing beat.
2. **The dinner is placed at 0:45.** CFA already flagged it: SB-07 belongs with Verse 3. Keep it there. Nothing earlier should show Vader; the dinner is the trap springing, and a trap seen at 0:45 is not a trap.
3. **Five choruses, one picture.** SB-02, SB-08 and SB-14 are three variants of "Dolo performs." The song's choruses are not the same chorus: the first is a price, the three middle ones ask the question (why did I turn around), the final one answers it with the price paid. Each chorus needs the same setup with one change, so the repetition reads as a change in the man, not a cut back to B-roll. Section 2 lays this out.
4. **The turnaround is the hinge and the board makes it a ship shot.** SB-21 is a spacecraft banking. The hinge of the whole story is a hand that has not hit the gas and a copilot's look. SB-20 and SB-23 are closer to it. The ship exterior is the consequence, not the decision.
5. **"Told the kid good luck" is drawn as Dolo in the rain (SB-22).** The kid is not in the cast and should not become a face. A comms display, a distant craft or Dolo's hand cutting a channel carries that line. SB-22 can survive as Dolo's wet exit at the ramp, but it is not the kid.
6. **SB-30 invents a thaw.** The lyric arc ends with the price paid, the copilot's promise and two words. A reunion after the freeze answers a question the song leaves open and softens the sacrifice. I'd cut it from the video and keep it for the frame story (section 4), where it belongs.
7. **SB-33 and SB-35 are outro ideas, not shots.** The outro's three names (the other production, the outer-world voice, the shout-out) are acknowledgements. A desert and a title card both make them literal. I'd treat the outro as a restrained performance image with Chewie holding the room, and let the names arrive as sound and edit, not as new locations.
8. **The bounty beat is thin.** SB-09 (the puck) and SB-11 (the alley) are good, but the first verse names two creditors, Jabba's funding and the hunters, and the board gives the hunters no panel of their own: a puck and Dolo fleeing. One Boba beat in the bay (already S02B) is enough; it needs to be a held, motionless watch, not a chase.
9. **The grill-change board (the ten quarters panels) is a different scene.** It is a getting-ready sequence, and the iced look with the rings out is a scoped alternate, not the video's default mouth. It does not belong inside the song. It belongs before it. See section 4.

## 2. The spine: shot to shot

The rule I'm writing to: **a few recurring setups, each returning changed, with purpose-built inserts between them.** The video has four recurring frames and three cutting motifs.

Recurring frames: **A** the counter (the locked medium-wide from the opening prototype: Dolo, four credits, the indicator, an implied customer); **B** the cockpit portrait (Dolo in the pilot seat, Chewie's shoulder or hands at the edge of frame); **C** the boards (Chewie at the console, hands on a fader or lever, the room behind him); **D** the chamber (cool light, steam, the slab).

Cutting motifs: **hands on money become hands on controls** (count, fader, throttle, lever, the slab's raised hands); **Chewie's look** (amused, skeptical, trusted); **the mouth** (open on it, close on it; grill off throughout the song by rule, grill up only in the frame story).

## 3. Scene cards to detail next

1. **S07, "Swing her back."** The dramatic hinge. Proves the copilot's look, a hand-on-control insert and a mouth beat in one scene. Ingredients: frame B starting still (cockpit, current Dolo, external-off mouth), Chewie reference (Chewbacca anatomy, not the ape), the case-of-credits prop, the distress display as an unlettered light. ## 4. The frame: why this video happens

Ziggy's larger idea is that the music video sits inside a scene with context, and that real life resumes after it. Three ways to do that, each using material that already exists.

**Option A, the fare (before the song).** The opening prototype has an implied offscreen customer. Ziggy has just added an unnamed informant as a new passenger who gets into a cab and interacts with Dolo. Make her the customer. The frame before the song: she boards (the four-beat cab storyboard already prepared, external-off mouth and lip hoops exactly as its brief states), the cab brings her to the counter, she has information and no money, and the negotiation is the first chorus. The video is Dolo's pitch to one specific person. Her name and backstory stay open; this gives her a job without inventing a biography.

**Option C, the quarters (after the song).** The ten grill-change panels Grok drafted are not a music-video beat and, because they end with the iced grill in and the lip rings out, they cannot sit before a song whose mouth is external-off by rule without a second mirror beat the board does not have. They work as the epilogue instead. After the slab: a short thaw (the only place SB-30 belongs), then Dolo alone in his quarters, rings out, case open, the iced grill in, the mirror reveal, the turn-back exit. He is getting ready to charge somebody again. The iced look stays scoped to that scene, which is exactly the scope CFA recorded for it, and nothing follows it that needs the other mouth. The sequence needs its continuity repairs first (CFA's five priorities), but as story it is already right, and it gives the whole piece its last ironic beat: the man who paid is back in business.

**Option B, the message.** Dolo records the track in the club booth with Chewie on the decks as a message left for Leia before the dinner. The video is the memory he leaves her; after it, she hears it. Honest, but it tells the audience he knew the dinner was a trap, which the plan says not to imply, and it makes the greed a performance. I'd not use it.

**Recommendation:** A into the song into C. Cab (the informant boards, external-off per her brief) → the counter (the fare; the first chorus begins) → the video → thaw → quarters (grill up; the iced look's own scene) → end on the mirror. Chewie keeping his promise can be a beat inside the thaw, with the informant as the witness if Ziggy wants her to return. One mouth change happens in the whole piece, and it happens on camera in the quarters. All of it is Ziggy's call; none of it is in any ledger yet.

## 5. Between the music: stories we could weave

The engine of the character is one sentence: **he always charges, and he always ends up paying.** That is a series engine, not a single-video engine. Seeds, in the order I'd develop them:

1. **"No Extra Charge."** The turnaround as a short on its own: the clean exit, the look, the swing back, told in real time with the song reduced to score. The same cast, the same sets, a second deliverable from the first production's assets.
2. **"The Informant."** Her story. What she knows, who she sold it to before Dolo, and why a man who charges for everything gives her a free ride. This is the natural place for the cab, the city, the hover traffic, and it keeps Leia out of it, which keeps the music video's ending intact.
3. **"Carbon Knight."** The time inside the ice, told from Chewie's side: a copilot keeping a promise to a woman who talks too much. A Chewie-led short needs no lip-sync for the lead, which is a production advantage as much as a story one.
4. **"The Bosses."** The boss discovery round (slug, hippo, gecko-frog) is a casting exercise today. It is also a client list. One job per boss, one episode per job, one price per episode.
5. **"Grill Up."** The quarters scene as a vignette: five minutes, no dialogue, a man and a mirror. It already has a storyboard.

Two boundaries I'd hold. First, the outro's nod to the other production is an acknowledgement, not a crossover; Jet Black is Lisan's guide and the welcome keeps the two casts apart, so any shared-universe device (an outer-world broadcast both productions hear, say) needs Ziggy to decide it on purpose. Second, the kid stays offscreen in this video; if an anthology ever casts him, that is a new decision with its own references.
