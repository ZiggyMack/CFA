## The event arc (every event placed)

1. **The clock** — the brown sky, the storm clock turning. Dread establishes.
2. **The ladder down** — descent into the dark.
3. **The line cleared** — grit to the elbow, choked line worked free.
4. **The valve freed** — seized valve broken with iron.
5. **The climb** — up the shaft.
6. **The door that moves** — a shoulder to a door that has not moved in forty years. This is the *build's* peak, **not** the release.
7. **The storm hits the cliff like a wave** — outside, the storm wall throws itself against the stone.
8. **The breath** — the vault breathes air instead of sand. **The release begins here.**
9. **The lamps** — the pump's dim lamps come up. **The release lands here** (with the breath), not on the door.
10. **The water** — the release settles into flow.

Mood: dread under work, then one release that is relief and not triumph.

## Emitters the music must respect

The pump's dim lamps (low, warm, flickering), a child's hand lamp (small, fragile, close), the lattice above him (structural, high; its colour is unrecorded, so the music invents no colour). The music leaves room for these lights rather than washing over them.

## Direction A — dread under work / the machine breathes

A low mechanical-industrial score. A distant clock-like pulse marks the storm clock; sub-bass drones hold the brown sky; dry metallic clanks and scrapes carry the grit and the iron (the line, the valve, the climb); the door is a grinding low swell that strains but does not open into relief; the storm is a distant wave of low noise outside the stone. Then the release: a soft exhale of breath-like pads as the vault breathes air, warm low tones rising as the lamps come up, and a gentle trickle for the water. Build to one relief, never triumph.

## Direction B — the vault's silence / chamber dread

A sparse ambient chamber score, dread carried in the negative space. Near-silence with a barely audible clock tick; the work sounds come as distant, muffled metallic tones (the grit, the valve, the climb) rather than foreground percussion; the door is a single low creak in the dark; the storm is a remote rumble through the stone. The release is a slow warm chord blooming like the lamps, air as a breath, water as a faint high shimmer. Relief, not triumph.
