**The story, in one paragraph.** A man who charges for everything is paid in information. Before the song (the prologue, P-1 to P-7), he drives a cab; a woman with a tip and no fare boards, tells him once where a woman with money will be, and he drops her and walks into the cantina where rides are sold. The song starts as he settles at the counter. The first chorus (S01) is his price, sold across the counter to a customer who stays off-screen. The first verse (S02) is the creditors behind the boast. The song goes on through the ship, the chase, a dinner with a creditor he fears, and ends (S10) with the woman across the counter telling him she loves him; the epilogue returns to the frame story and ends on a mirror. The audience is meant to enjoy a salesman enjoying a customer's reluctance; the humour is played straight; the look is photoreal performance, not animation.

**The intent for this sequence.** Everything in the cab is small: one passenger, one glance, one half-smile. Everything at the counter is the same man at full volume. The cut between them is the song starting. The one performance thread shared by the two scenes is the half-smile: it ends the cab's glance (P-5) and it ends the counter's pitch (S01-3). The music enters under the last prologue frame or the first song frame (an open choice, section C), and the first word of the song lands on a cut to his mouth.

**The characters in this sequence.** Dolo: the man; brown skin; a dark brown distressed leather jacket over an open off-white shirt; the identity locks below. Chewie: his partner, a dark gorilla-like beast with black over-ear headphones, a gold neck chain and a bullet bandolier, lean build, seen at the cantina's boards (the DJ beast sheet is his established reference since 2026-10-12).
