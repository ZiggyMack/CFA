## Logline

A young man whose people ration a dying well does the one thing their Keepers forbid, walks onto the open sand and calls the worm, and finds a watcher waiting on the dune with a blueprint anyone can read. The watcher can show him how the well works; he cannot turn the valve. Lisan turns it, and then he shows everyone.

## The theme, in one paragraph

The Keepers keep the knowing, and they are not villains. They keep it because the last time it was shared, a generation ago, the man who shared it had half of it: he opened the scoop in a storm with nobody told how the baffle worked, the vault drank sand for a season, and three people died of the dry. The Keepers sealed the scoop, and the knowing with it. Restraint is what you do with half a blueprint. **[new, on Ziggy's direction of October 13: the Keepers' reason. The argument in the story is not sharing against keeping; it is whole against half.]** The blueprint cannot be kept, because it is light: whoever looks at it has it. Jet is the one who brings it and explains it; he never does the work, and his gloved hand never touches the pump. **[proposed rule: Jet shows and never does. The record says Lisan chooses and acts and lists Jet's powers as unestablished; the rule is this story's, and the three later lines that state it follow from it.]** Lisan is the one who acts, and then the one who brings the truth, the knowledge and the way of thinking to the people in front of him, and the change happens in a child, a woman and an old man, each by their own hands. That is the real team's intention mirrored as fiction: shared models, shared blueprints, shared instructions. It is a story, not a claim about the world and not an authorization to share real files.

## The story

### 1. The vault [C18 water-cistern-vault]

Under the cliff settlement there is a vault, and in the vault there is a pump older than anyone's grandmother. Its lamps are dim. Three Keepers stand between the people and the pump and fill the cups: half for a child, a third for a man, a swallow for the old. Lisan is in the line with his hood down. On his throat, under the jaw, two worms with their mouths open toward each other and a sun with an eye between them; between his brows the two small letters; down his chin the four. The Keepers call those marks the dead sign. His mother wore them. **[new: the settlement, the Keepers, the rationing, the marks as an old sign of the settlement, Lisan's mother.]**

He asks the oldest Keeper why the pump gives less every season. The Keeper fills the next cup. "The knowing is ours. The water is yours. That is the arrangement." **[proposed dialogue]**

### 2. The rack [C12 cliff-sietch]

Night. The settlement's cliff face, the ladders, the shuttered openings. On a rack by the lowest ladder, the thumpers: iron stakes with a spring hammer in the head, used by the Keepers' fathers and by nobody now, because the open sand is the worm's and the worm is death. Lisan takes one. He fits the breather under his nostrils, the one thin tube across his right cheek into his hair, and goes down.

### 3. The tracks [C15 worm-tracks-thumper]

Dawn, flat white light. The open sand beyond the last rock, crossed by old furrows wider than a road. He walks to where three furrows cross, drives the thumper into the sand with both hands, and lets go. The hammer begins. He stands beside it with nothing in his hands. *(One action: the planting. The hero frame for this scene is a C15 draft with the thumper in his hands and the tracks behind.)*

### 4. The rise [C13 sandworm-rising; landing on panel LAG-SC-10's burst]

The sand answers. First a ridge, far off, moving. Then the haze lifts and the maw rises out of it behind him, wider than the vault, ringed with teeth, sand pouring from its lip. He does not run and he does not turn. He holds, one breath, eyes ahead, the thumper beating beside him. *(This is the sandworm card's SW-2 and the first clip of Track A; the two tracks share it.)*

The sand takes the frame. When it clears he is coming up out of it, spitting grit, the worm's furrow fresh beside him and the thumper gone. He is alive because he stood still. **[proposed reading: the worm passed; he went under; he surfaced. The record says nothing about the worm's behavior; this is story.]**

On the dune behind him stands a man who was not there. **[proposed staging; no arrival power is established in the record]**

A black suit in the desert, cut close. Polished shoes on the sand. Where the head should be, a clear sphere with a nebula turning in it, gold and cyan. Beside his left hand, which is gloved, a small cube turns in the air. His right hand is bare, and it is the blue of the sphere. He has not moved. He did not help. *(Composition inspiration: the received arrival-in-sand frame 048b71751cb2 and the desert meeting ccd25e07763a, both unapproved; Jet's build per REF-02.)*

LISAN: You watched.
JET: That is what I am for. **[proposed dialogue]**

### 5. The meeting [desert, between the furrows; composition after ccd25e07763a]

Jet comes down the dune the way a man walks, no faster. He stops at the distance one stops from a stranger with a knife, though Lisan has none. The sphere has no face to read, so the voice does the work. It is plain, and it says what is.

JET: I was sent to you. Not to the Keepers. To you.
LISAN: Sent by who?
JET: The ones who made this. **[the sender stays open in the record; the line keeps it open]**

He raises the bare hand and the cube opens. Not a door, not a weapon: a lattice of light the size of a man, lines and joints, turning slowly in the air between them. **[new, proposed form of the blueprint: a lattice of light that shows how a thing works, that anyone who looks at it keeps, that cannot be owned because it is light. The record calls a cube or map display a candidate visual language, not a locked mechanism; this is the candidate.]**

Lisan knows the shape before he knows he knows it. It is the pump. The valve that seizes every season; the line behind it choked with sand; above it, closed for forty years, the wind-scoop that was built to breathe the vault clean.

JET: The valve. The line. The scoop. Anyone who looks at this has it. That is what it is for.
LISAN: Then turn it.
JET: I can show you. I cannot turn it.

He says it without apology. The gloved hand stays at his side. It will stay there the whole film.

LISAN: Then show me again. Slower.

*(Here Lisan chooses. The record's JET-03, "Lisan chooses the next action", is unplaced; this is the proposal for it: the first mission is the pump, the first action is the walk back with the knowing.)*

### 6. The clock [C09 sandstorm-wall]

They walk back toward the cliff with the lattice folded into the cube. On the horizon behind them the sky has gone brown from the ground up: the storm wall, two hours out. When it hits the cliff the vault will breathe sand, and a choked line with a seized valve will not open again. Jet does not say any of that. Lisan says it, and starts to run.

### 7. The vault, again [C18; the set piece]

The Keepers at the pump. Lisan comes down the ladder with a stranger behind him whose head is a sky, and the line of people parts the way a line parts for a man with a dead sign on his throat.

KEEPER: The knowing is ours. The last man who opened that scoop killed three of us.
LISAN: He opened it in a storm with half of it. One of the three was my mother. I have the whole of it, and the storm is two hours out.

The Keeper does not answer, because the Keeper has never seen the whole of it. **[new; proposed dialogue. The Keeper's objection is true, and the story lets it be true.]** Lisan goes under the pump. The lattice hangs above him, turning, and a child holds the lamp. He clears the line by hand, grit to the elbow. He frees the valve with the thumper's iron twin from the rack. He climbs the shaft to the scoop and puts his shoulder to a door that has not moved in forty years, and it moves. Above, the storm hits the cliff like a wave. Below, the vault breathes: not sand, air, drawn down through the scoop the way it was built to be. The pump's lamps come up. The water comes up.

Jet stands in the doorway through all of it. Both hands at his sides. The gloved one, when the Keeper looks at it, is empty.

The oldest Keeper looks up at the lattice. It is still there. It has not gone with the stranger into the cube. It can be looked at by anyone in the room, and the old man understands, late and completely, that it always could have been, and that the half he kept for forty years had kept the scoop shut through forty storms, and could not have opened it in one. **[new, on Ziggy's direction of October 13: the old man's understanding; the lattice staying visible follows the labeled proposal in scene 5.]**

### 8. The fire [C31 tribe-gathering; C25 campfire-wasteland]

Night, the storm gone over, the settlement at the fire with full cups. Lisan opens the lattice over the fire for everyone. A child draws the valve in the sand with a finger and gets it right. A woman asks about the second line, the one to the lower vault, and Lisan does not know, and the oldest Keeper does, and says so, out loud, to all of them. **[the transformation "inside every man, woman and child" as action: the child learns, the woman asks, the Keeper teaches.]**

Jet comes back to the edge of the firelight, the way he came to the dune: he is there. His first report. **[JET-04, the record's campfire staging f59b02d5c59b as composition inspiration]**

JET: They saw your lamps from the tower. Someone answered.
LISAN: Someone where?
JET: Out there. **[the outer world; the sender and the wording stay open]**

### 9. The tower [C36 radio-tower-wastes; C24 night-desert-stars; C43 rig-rooftop-sentinel for Jet]

Lisan climbs the dead radio tower on the ridge, a thing the Keepers also kept. The lattice shows him the relay; he throws the key. Noise. Then, inside the noise, a light far out on the dunes, blinking in the same rhythm his lamps blinked when the pump came up. Another settlement. Another vault. Somebody out there looked at the same light. **[proposed: the answer is a second settlement; the dispatch's content stays Ziggy's]**

Below him, on the roof of a rusted rig, Jet stands watching the horizon, the cube turning by his gloved hand. **[JET-05 staging: the watcher's post]**

Last frame: two hands. Lisan's on the key, grit still under the nails. Jet's, gloved, at his side.
