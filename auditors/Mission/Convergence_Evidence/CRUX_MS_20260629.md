## Classification

**Type:** Stochastic — intra-auditor definitional instability

**DBEP Layer:** Definitions (intra-auditor) — Claude cycled between two legitimate definitions of "Moral Substance" mid-deliberation, producing oscillation rather than a stable position.

**Definition 1 (high score):** Moral Substance = metaphysical grounding of objective morality in divine nature. CT grounds moral facts in God's nature → high moral substance.

**Definition 2 (low score):** Moral Substance = measurable moral outcomes and behavioral guidance. CT's moral claims are contested, culturally variable, and not empirically validated → low moral substance.

These are not competing beliefs about CT — they are competing definitions of the metric. Claude's teleological lens activates both readings depending on framing, producing oscillation.

---

## Why This Is NOT a Structural Crux

A structural crux represents a stable philosophical impasse where both auditors have considered the same question and genuinely cannot converge. MS does not meet this standard:

1. **Failed to reproduce reliably** — 3/7 hardcoded, 1/10 external. If the impasse were philosophical, it would be consistent across runs.
2. **Grok was stable** — Grok's steady ~5.0 reflects a consistent empirical view (CT's moral framework lacks falsifiable grounding but has historical moral influence). Grok was not in a crux — Claude was.
3. **Temperature-dependent** — The oscillation pattern is sensitive to sampling temperature, not fixed philosophical disagreement.
4. **Intra-auditor, not inter-auditor** — True crux requires stable positions from both auditors. Claude's position was unstable; Grok's was not.

---

## The Real Finding

MS reveals a metric that activates different definitional frames in Claude's teleological lens depending on how the question is approached. This is distinct from IP's problem (two auditors using different definitions) — this is one auditor cycling through two definitions within a single deliberation.

**Implication for profile writing:** MS scores from runs where Claude oscillated should be treated with caution. The "final score" of 1.0 in H1 reflects the last position Claude landed on after oscillating, not a settled evaluation.
