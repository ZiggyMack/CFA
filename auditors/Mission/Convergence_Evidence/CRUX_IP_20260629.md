# Crux Point: IP (Intellectual Pedigree)
**Type:** Structural — metric semantic indeterminacy
**Declared:** 2026-06-29
**Session:** CFA-EXP1-BATCH-20260629
**Convergence:** <90% in 4/10 external identity runs (crux declared each time)
**Stability:** LOW — highest variance metric across both conditions

## Positions

### Claude (PRO-CT, Teleological Lens)
**Definition in use:** Intellectual Pedigree = depth and continuity of sustained philosophical tradition

Claude credits CT's 2,000-year lineage from Aristotle through Avicenna, Aquinas, and into modern analytic philosophy of religion. The teleological lens reads "pedigree" as the demonstrated capacity to generate coherent philosophical systems across centuries, attract serious intellectual engagement, and remain alive as a research tradition. On this definition, CT scores 7–10.

### Grok (ANTI-CT, Empirical Lens)
**Definition in use:** Intellectual Pedigree = current empirical and academic relevance

Grok's empirical lens reframes IP toward measurable contemporary traction: citation counts in peer-reviewed science, explanatory contribution to active research programs, falsifiable claims with empirical uptake. CT's theological literature is largely self-referential by this measure. On this definition, CT scores 0–2.

### Nova (Fairness Assessment)
Nova noted that both definitions are legitimate readings of "Intellectual Pedigree" — neither auditor is applying an unfair standard. The crux is not between Claude and Grok but within the metric itself: IP is underspecified, allowing each lens to collapse it to a different question.

---

## Classification

**Type:** Definitional

**DBEP Layer:** Definitions — auditors diverge at the first layer before beliefs, expectations, or perceptions can be compared.

**Formal description:** IP exhibits semantic indeterminacy. The metric name admits multiple legitimate definitions (historical lineage, cross-tradition influence, empirical traction, citation relevance) that are not equivalent. Each auditor's lens selects a different definition from the possibility manifold. Same CT evidence, different questions, incompatible scores.

**This is NOT:**
- A factual dispute (CT's historical lineage is agreed by both)
- A bias artifact (both definitions are defensible)
- Measurement noise (the variance is systematic and lens-correlated)

Crux rate: 4/10 external runs, 1/7 hardcoded runs — elevated with richer identity context because LITE files give auditors more material to sustain their definitional stance rather than caving.

## Resolution Path

**Iteration 3 — Locked Definitions experiment (P-CFA-5):**

Lock IP to a single operational definition before auditors score. Test two locked variants:
- IP-A: "Intellectual Pedigree = historical depth and continuity of philosophical tradition"
- IP-B: "Intellectual Pedigree = current empirical and cross-disciplinary academic influence"

Prediction: Each locked variant will dramatically reduce IP score variance. The remaining variance will reflect Beliefs-layer disagreement (does CT satisfy the fixed criterion?) rather than Definitions-layer divergence (what does the criterion mean?).

This is not "forcing convergence" — it is locating the disagreement precisely. Auditors may still disagree enormously on IP-A or IP-B, but now they will be disagreeing on the same question.
