## 1. Notes on the board as it stands (story level)

The fifty panels are a persona collage. From shot to shot the man is a chef, a doctor, a lawyer, a DJ, an engineer, a prophet, a voodoo doctor, a festival act, a man with a dog, a man with a ring. Each panel is keyed to a lyric cue, and the guide's own arc reads: reflection in the chorus; silence, burial, the grind and the trial by fire in the bridge and first verse; creation and community in the second; inward work, voodoo and chaos in the third, ending at the two doors; then the mic and the signal. That arc is good, and the panels serve it one at a time.

What the board does not have is a body that stays. By the guide's own count, the signature look (tan stillsuit, black hooded cloak, gold Cuban chain) appears in panels 01 and 49 only; everything between changes wardrobe, setting and lighting at every cut. Forty-nine of fifty panels are reviewed at contact-sheet level only, and Grok's own QA reports drift on most of them (AL doubled or on a cheek, GAIB repeated or slid, rings on the wrong brow, hoops in both ears). None of that is a story fault. It is what you get when fifty prompts each answer one line. The fault, for a video, is that there is no "him" to return to between the lines. The board is a mirror, which is the song's title, and a mirror needs someone standing in front of it.

The LAG-1K archive is the opposite object. Nine hundred and fifty-three drafts in forty-three categories, one man, one wardrobe, one world: dune sea, salt flats, convoys, wrecks, storms, ruins, a sietch, a canyon fort, tribe gatherings, a worm rising. The team rules say it outright: "Mad Max desert scope only." Nobody made these for the fifty panels; they were made as a world. Read side by side, the two sets are the two halves of one film, and the board's own hinge panels already say so. Panel 01: he speaks into an antique mirror, and the mirror shows the desert sun. Panel 48: "outer world, inner world, two doors" (cue, reported).

## 2. The spine: two worlds, one face

**The thesis.** Two lines run the whole song. The A-line is the desert, the outer world: his body is there from the first bar to the last, in the signature look, walking, listening, kneeling, calling the worm, facing the storm. The B-line is the mirror, the inner world: the fifty panels, where he is everyone. He enters the B-line through a mirror and leaves it through one. Every chorus returns to a mirror. Every verse goes out into the desert and cuts into the mirror for the hats. The finale puts the mirror's sun into the desert's sky, which panel 50 already does.

This changes nothing in the sources. The fifty panels keep their numbers, cues and placeholder holds; the Jet record's scope flags stay as they are (no source panel replaced, no lyric changed), and the scene map's note that the fifty scenes, cues and timing fields are unchanged still holds. What the thesis decides is what the desert footage is *for*, and therefore which LAG-1K categories get shortlisted first and what HaL is asked to refine. If Ziggy reads the song differently, the map in section 5 re-sorts in an hour.

## 3. Jet Black, placed (proposal)

## 5. Reuse: what the LAG-1K already gives

The archive on D: holds 953 drafts in 43 categories under `references/From Grok/GROK-LAG-1K-*` (24 standalone packages at 22 to 24 drafts each, plus the G1 package with 19 categories and 419 drafts). C14 and C26 do not exist as Lisan packages. There is no shortlist yet and no catalog entry; HaL's shortlist is requested at Ziggy's go, and CFA Nova's catalog is where the set becomes reviewable. `LAG1K_STORY_MAP.md` lists every category with the spine section it serves and the team's own preliminary picks from the NOTES files, so the shortlist starts where the story needs it. The picks there are the bots' self-scores; I have viewed five drafts.

What the whole archive teaches, from the NOTES and from the five I viewed:

- **The lettering and the worms are the hard parts.** GAIB drops its I (GAB, GIB) in a large share of drafts; it goes horizontal or repeats down the throat; AL rides up the forehead. The neck worms come out as snakes, cobras or horns, raised and three-dimensional in dark, wide and backlit shots; the eye-disc comes out as a plain sun. The team's own tips (eye first, "four letters, the I included", "flat black ink", "Dune-style worms, not snakes") are the right prompt discipline and should be the repair brief for any shortlisted frame; on framing the bots disagree (front-facing chest-up held best in the C40 and C01 notes, while one bot reports that three-quarter views keep GAIB vertical), so framing is a per-shot choice, not a rule.
- **The silver piercings are absent from the desert.** The team's base identity text (TEAM-RULES line 4) names the face, the curls, the eyes, AL, GAIB, the worms, the disc and natural teeth, and does not name the nostril hoop, brow ring or ear hoop. All five drafts I viewed have none. The fifty panels mostly have them (sometimes on the wrong side). This is a decision, not a defect to repair blindly: either the desert is where he wears none, or the shortlist gets a bounded repair pass. Ziggy's call (section 7).
- **The eyes drift pale.** The two C15 drafts I viewed have grey and brown eyes against the reference's electric blue, and C35-15 reads brown too; the NOTES report the same across categories. A likeness check for any desert frame must include eye color.
- **The wardrobe mostly holds.** The stillsuit and cloak are stable through the archive; C13-20 drifts to a dark hooded jacket, C13-08 adds layered chains and a strap. Record under Outfit, separately from likeness.
