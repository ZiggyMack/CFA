REVIEW CARD — SAME QUESTIONS FOR EVERY RESULT
Watch the original in normal-speed playback, then inspect the start, middle, end, finger movement and any visible defect. Use neutral review labels A/B/C/... before revealing provider names where practical. Native watermarks may make blinding incomplete; retain them and note the limit.
Score each supported category 0-4: 0 unusable/broken, 1 major defects, 2 mixed/repair needed, 3 usable with minor defects, 4 clean for this shot. Use N/A for a feature not exposed; do not infer grill fidelity from closed lips or full-body continuity from a medium shot.
- Identity and scoped details: face/hair, lettering, visible tattoo shapes, rings and visible teeth.
- Hands and objects: anatomy, finger movement, four stable rectangular credits and consistent scale/contact if any occurs.
- Temporal continuity: flicker, morphing, duplication, disappearing pieces, wardrobe/set drift.
- Instruction-following: specified gaze/hand sequence, fixed camera, one person, no invented words/cuts.
- Cinematic usefulness: readable intention, natural performance, usable beginning/end and visual quality at native size.
For every defect, record the time/frame, severity and source comparison. A serious identity substitution or impossible hand/credit event fails reusable-shot acceptance even if the clip looks beautiful. Keep the individual scores and Ziggy's preference; do not hide tradeoffs in one grand winner number.

Also record actual duration/resolution/FPS; requested versus delivered output count; request/queue/completion times where observable; credits/currency and whether estimated or actually attributed; retries; operator effort; download integrity; and real model/version/graph evidence. Subscription credits are not automatically comparable dollars. Unknown values remain unknown. A new generation failure is an outcome to retain, not a reason to quietly buy another attempt.
