**The story, in one paragraph.** A man who charges for everything is paid in information. Before the song (the prologue, P-1 to P-7), he drives a cab; a woman with a tip and no fare boards, cannot pay, tells him once where a woman with money will be who needs a ride, and he accepts the information as her fare, drops her, crosses the street and walks into the cantina where rides are sold. The song starts on the cut to the cantina room. The first chorus (S01) is his price, sold across the counter to a customer who stays off-screen. The first verse (S02) is the creditors behind the boast. The song goes on through the ship, the chase, a dinner with a creditor he fears and a freezing he did not order; in its last verse (S10) the woman across the counter tells him she loves him; the final chorus (S11) sets the freezing itself against him performing about it on the ship; the outro (S12) closes on the mouth; the epilogue returns to the frame story and ends on a mirror. The audience is meant to enjoy a salesman enjoying a customer's reluctance; the humour is played straight; the look is photoreal performance, not animation.

**The intent for this sequence.** Everything in the cab is small: one passenger, one glance, one half-smile. Everything at the counter is the same man at full volume. The music enters on the cut to the room wide (fixed), and the first word of the song lands on a cut to his mouth (fixed); frame A follows (fixed).
