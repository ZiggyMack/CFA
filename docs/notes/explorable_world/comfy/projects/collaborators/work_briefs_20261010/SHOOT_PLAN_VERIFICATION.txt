CFA STORYBOARD SHOOT-PLAN VERIFICATION
Recorded by CFA Codex Nova, October 10, 2026, from Ziggy's direct workshop guidance.

PURPOSE
A character ready for a scene does not make that scene ready to shoot. Review
how the approved ingredients will perform and be photographed before a
production handoff. This is separate from accepting a generated result.

ORDER
1. Choose the story beat, cast, setting, costume and exact scoped references.
2. Develop the panel and required ingredients; record unresolved defects.
3. Verify the direction and cinematography in the shoot plan.
4. Review the complete scene against the B-or-better production-handoff target.
5. Prepare the bounded provider/workflow handoff and its execution preflight.
No stage, grade or planning document proves generation, continuity or acceptance.

TWO REVIEW PASSES
Direction: describe action beats, blocking and positions, emotional intention,
performance, eyelines, who reacts to whom, and what the viewer must understand.
Keep the approved identity and costume. Raise a necessary change as a scoped
question; do not silently redesign them during verification.

Cinematography: choose shot size, framing, camera position and angle, movement,
start/end composition, subject focus, lighting direction and mood, and continuity
across cuts. State how each choice supports the beat. Avoid a list of attractive
camera terms that cannot coexist in one short shot. Any runtime/model limitation
remains a separate execution check, with unknowns recorded as unknown.

A continuity review can check screen direction, eyelines, pose, hands, props,
costume and lighting between adjoining shots. A third collaborator need not be
forced into a new specialty solely because three collaborators are available.

WHAT TO SUPPLY
Stable project/scene ID and story beat; current panel; ingredient/reference list
with exact IDs, paths/hashes, approved roles, likeness ratings and known
exceptions; relevant neighboring shots; intended duration if known; existing
material to reuse. Do not claim visual review from filenames or text summaries.
A chat-only participant can return an authored text plan through the attributed
courier; the courier does not make that participant a filesystem operator.

RETURN FOR EACH SCENE
- Direction: action, blocking, eyelines, performance and reactions.
- Camera: framing, angle, movement, start/end composition and cut continuity.
- Lighting: source/direction, exposure focus, mood and continuity.
- Specific input/reference choices and any scene-only exception.
- Required ingredients: Ready, Needs fixes, Missing or Unreviewed, with notes.
- Critical blockers and the next bounded repair for each.
- Scene grade: A, B, C, D, F or Ungraded, with a reviewed explanation.
- Shoot-plan review: Unreviewed, Needs fixes or Verified; actual reviewer and time.
- One coherent draft prompt consistent with the reviewed direction and camera.

HANDOFF RULE
B or better is a target, not a mean of image stars. Every required ingredient
must be reviewed Ready, with valid reference bindings or reviewed non-media
criteria. No critical blocker may remain. The shoot plan must be documented
and actually marked Verified with reviewer attribution. Good lighting cannot
cancel missing fingers or an unfinished required character. Unknown stays
Unreviewed/Ungraded. A grade never changes character locks or accepts a render.
Drafts and repair requests remain useful while production handoff is blocked.

REFERENCE RULE
Image stars are 1-5 likeness to the gold standard for the recorded role, not
likability. Prime keeps a look in play. An explicitly chosen lower-rated image
is a scene-only choice; other scenes return to the canonical scoped gold
standards and highest-rated suitable approved references. Do not propagate a
scene exception into a new default character. Use the library's REFERENCE_POLICY.txt.
