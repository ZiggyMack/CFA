THE ROLES
Casting: develop/select character candidates and preserve the scoped identity, components and creative lane.
Writing: turn the song/story into meaningful scene beats and scene cards.
Direction: intention, action, blocking, performance, eyelines and dramatic rhythm.
Cinematography: framing, angle, movement, lens intent, light sources and continuity.
Set design: scene geography, atmosphere and story-bearing background details.
Props: correct objects, counts, materials, interaction and continuity.
Costume: select wardrobe early; preserve approved construction without redesigning the actor during shoot-plan verification.
VFX: effects and finishing with explicit preservation goals and measured results.
Continuity: identity, mouth, tattoos, jewelry, hands, props, space and temporal joins.
You may cover more than one role. We will not invent a third role merely to occupy a third AI, or assign roles from tool count alone.

HOW WE LEARN
Use real project work. Start with a scoped brief, exact inputs, a bounded deliverable and a clear success check. Keep the first independent attempt. Record what you could do alone and what blocked you before consulting teammates or receiving courier help. Then let a teammate help and save the assisted result separately. Rotate roles when the project benefits: a later scene can give another collaborator the director or lighting brief. This should improve the actual film while revealing who is effective at each role.

Grade the role-specific deliverable, with reviewer/date and actual evidence. Track quality, consistency, measured time, repair effort, observed cost and tool limits separately. Unknown stays unknown. A chat-only director can write a strong plan even without a render button; direct rendering and creative planning are different capabilities. Team output needs named contributors and dependencies. A single good image does not establish a success rate or an all-purpose winner.

THE THREE DIFFERENT REVIEWS
1. Asset likeness: the user's 1-5 stars compare an asset to its scoped gold standard; they do not measure personal preference or provider skill. Highest relevant likeness is the default identity starting point unless a scene explicitly requests another variant. A scene-specific exception never silently becomes the global reference.
2. Storyboard/scene readiness: review required ingredients, critical defects and the shoot plan. The character can be production-ready while its scene is not. Direction, camera and lighting must have an actual verification record before a production handoff. B or better is the user's target, and an unresolved critical defect still blocks shooting. Draft/repair handoffs can happen earlier.
3. Collaborator role score: assess that collaborator's actual contribution as casting, writing, direction, cinematography or another named role. Asset stars and a scene's grade never automatically become an AI's grade.
