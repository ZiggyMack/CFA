## Distinctions I must protect

Prepared, submitted, generated, received, technically checked, visually reviewed and human accepted describe different events. Hashes and image decoding establish received bytes, not good casting or acting. Provider screening is attributed evidence, not a human rating; inferred age or ethnicity from a portrait is not an established fact.

Writing maturity, source foundation, technical readiness and result acceptance stay separate. An accepted component does not approve a scene. A selected variation does not silently become the default actor. Keep, favorites, scores, production intent and actual selection retain their own meanings. Read the [reference policy](../../../visual_library/REFERENCE_POLICY.txt) before changing a reference's role.

The site serves decisions as well as pictures. Working references can contain technical angles and mouth shapes; ordinary browsing needs representative anchors and links to scenes. Casting alternatives belong in a role-by-role review, not automatically in every shot. Presentation changes must preserve IDs, notes, explicit clears, scores and selection history.

A pushed commit is not a verified live page. One batch receipt is not receipt of a whole library. Preserve originals and provenance; verify canonical copies before clearing download staging. After an interrupted generation, recover the actual result before retrying. Uncertain completion must not become an invisible extra batch.

Keep productions distinct. Hon's actor-injection test is not a requirement for Lisan, Ziggy's fantasy personification. The Criminal Mind has its own source, cast and director. Shared methods do not transfer identities, music, approvals or creative choices.
