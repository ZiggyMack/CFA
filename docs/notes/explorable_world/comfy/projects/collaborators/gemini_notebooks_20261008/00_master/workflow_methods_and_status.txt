HOW TO CHOOSE
Begin with three concrete statements: the actual supplied file; the intended change; the features that must remain. Then use the workshop and problem-to-recipe lookup, consult the matching Panhandlers lesson, reuse an appropriate saved/official graph, and define one deliberate variable plus a success check. Separate workflows when models or production stages differ; combine compatible controls when it improves clarity.

Record reviewed, available, validated, executed and accepted separately. A saved graph is not a successful render. A successful decode is not a continuity pass. An aesthetically selected scene is not a technical or production acceptance. Cloud schemas/models are dated observations and must be checked again before relying on them.

CORE OPERATING LESSONS
Keep exact inputs, baseline graph/revision, effective compiled values and active outputs. Keep one deliberate change in an initial comparison. A fixed seed helps define a comparison; it is not an identity lock or portable equality guarantee across models or runtimes.
An ordinary KSampler bundles functions that some recipes expose separately. The Klein editor uses KSamplerSelect, SamplerCustomAdvanced, scheduler, guider and noise nodes. Do not bolt tutorial SDXL CFG/negative/denoise wiring onto a different architecture by name alone.
Prompt text, encoded conditioning and concatenated conditioning are different data. Inspect the active graph rather than assuming similarly labelled controls behave identically. In the distilled Klein baseline at CFG 1, the conventional negative field is inactive notes; positive wording carries the requested correction.
For an output comparison, copy sampler, decode and save together, inspect incoming links and give distinct output prefixes. Groups and collapsed subgraphs are organization, not off switches. Muting output branches, bypassing an optional adapter and changing queue/batch count are different actions.
Frames plus optional audio and source FPS can be assembled into VIDEO and saved. Existing VIDEO can expose images/audio/FPS through Get Video Components. Keep data types compatible. A canvas preview is not a retained playable file.
Changing only export FPS changes duration/speed; it does not generate intermediate frames or retime speech. Keep exact trim boundaries, frame counts, FPS, codec and measured audio/video durations with the result.
Keep replacement audio independent of generation. Listen to words actually produced; prompt text or enhancer output is not proof of what the audience will hear. A full mix, isolated vocal, reference voice and final soundtrack are different inputs.
Check source/output side by side at matched frames and normal speed. Inspect identity, tattoo spelling/side/geometry, grill and mouth motion, piercings, hands/contact, camera/cuts, background, flicker and audio. Record review limits honestly.
Accept the take before spending on finishing. Compare any upscale/regeneration against the retained original; more pixels do not prove recovered true detail or unchanged motion.

REUSABLE EVIDENCE TO RETURN
Problem; lesson; chosen recipe and why; baseline; one changed variable; exact input IDs/hashes; actual graph/model/settings; validation/run receipt; output ID/hash and measured media properties; observed defects; review scope; next decision. Keep unknown timing, cost and dependency fields unknown. Return reusable lessons through the manual CFA/Panhandlers handoff, not by silently rewriting old experiments.
