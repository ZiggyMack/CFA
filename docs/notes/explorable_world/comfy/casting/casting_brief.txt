WHAT GOOD REFERENCES LOOK LIKE
- Characters that feel filmed: natural skin, believable eyes, detailed hair/fur, realistic fabric and armor, plausible anatomy, and individual features.
- One unobscured character per machine-reference image. Use a plain backdrop and soft, neutral light for the master reference.
- A portrait for facial identity plus a separate full-body view for silhouette and costume. Show hands and footwear where appropriate; use anatomy-appropriate framing for creatures. A collage can support human review but is not the default ComfyUI input.
- Wardrobe with a readable silhouette. Test intricate patterns and loose accessories deliberately; their continuity in video is unproven.
- Keep the same selected identity and outfit across the reference set. Check facial structure, hairline, eyes, complexion, body shape, and distinguishing details after every generation.
- Treat Hon Dolo's tattoo placement as part of identity. Record each motif, size, orientation, and position using the character's own left/right. Flag mirrored, missing, or changed tattoos. Begin with a few readable motifs; any lettering must be specified and reviewed rather than improvised between frames.
- Save original files at the highest useful resolution the current interface provides. Record actual width and height; never infer them from words such as "8K" in a prompt.
